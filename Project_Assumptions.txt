Assignment 2 – Retail Analytics SQL Project Project Assumptions

1.  Revenue calculations

-   Gross line-item value is calculated as quantity multiplied by
    unit_price_at_sale.
-   Net line-item revenue is calculated as quantity multiplied by
    unit_price_at_sale multiplied by (1 - discount_rate).
-   Revenue calculations include only orders where order_status is
    ‘Delivered’, unless a question specifically asks about other order
    statuses.
-   Returned and cancelled orders are not included in delivered revenue.

2.  Order and customer counts

-   Each order is counted once when calculating the number of orders,
    even if the order contains multiple items.
-   Customers with no delivered orders are included in questions that
    explicitly ask for all customers or zero activity.
-   For the average customer net revenue in Q14, the average is
    calculated only across customers with delivered purchases, because
    the CTE includes customers with delivered orders.

3.  Product sales and ranking

-   Product sales revenue is calculated using the sale-time price
    (unit_price_at_sale), rather than the product’s current unit_price.
-   Discounts are applied using the discount_rate stored in order_items.
-   Products with no delivered sales should still appear in the product
    ranking query with net revenue shown as zero.
-   Product ranking is performed separately within each product
    category, with higher net revenue receiving a higher position.

4.  Stock classification

-   Products with stock_quantity below 20 are labelled ‘Low Stock’.
-   Products with stock_quantity from 20 through 40, inclusive, are
    labelled ‘Moderate Stock’.
-   Products with stock_quantity above 40 are labelled ‘High Stock’.

5.  Date handling

-   Q1 includes delivered orders from 1 October 2024 through 31 March
    2025, inclusive.
-   Monthly reporting groups orders by calendar year and month.
-   Monthly results are sorted chronologically, and running totals
    accumulate revenue in that order.

6.  Returned orders

-   Return analysis uses records in the Returns table to identify
    returned orders.
-   The returned order value is calculated before discounts, using
    quantity multiplied by unit_price_at_sale for each order item.

7.  Joins and missing records

-   INNER JOIN is used where the question requires matching records
    across the relevant tables.
-   LEFT JOIN is used where all records from the main table must be
    retained, including employees or customers with no matching orders.
-   NOT EXISTS is used to identify customers who have no delivered
    orders.

8.  Revenue contribution

-   The top-three-customer query ranks customers by net spend from
    delivered orders.
-   Each customer’s revenue percentage is calculated against total
    delivered net revenue across all customers with delivered purchases,
    not just the top three.

9.  Data and schema

-   The project uses the supplied dataset and the existing Customers,
    Products, Employees, Orders, Order_Items, and Returns tables.
-   Table and column names in the queries are based on the schema used
    in the PostgreSQL database.
-   No changes to the supplied data are assumed as part of the analysis.

Note: These assumptions describe how the assignment queries interpret
the supplied business rules and calculations. If the actual dataset or
database schema differs, the queries and assumptions should be reviewed
accordingly.
